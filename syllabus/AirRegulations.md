# Air Regulations Syllabus and Study Road Map

Sources used (all from `D:/Quiz App/build/raw_text/`): dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus.txt (DGCA CAR Section 7, Series B, Part IV, 16 June 2011), regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats.txt and regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats_2.txt (community reproductions of the same CAR outline), regulations_road_map_1.txt (SASTA Aviation Academy 20-day plan).

## Exam pattern

- The CAR states that applicants must pass a written examination based on the DGCA-prescribed syllabus for the Commercial Pilot's Licence (CPL), and for the Instrument Rating (IR) under Section O. Basis: Section J of Schedule II and Section O of Schedule II of the Aircraft Rules, 1937, with Rule 133A. (src: dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus; the same text appears in regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats_2)
- Air Regulations is one of the written-paper subjects. Its CAR section is Appendix C. (src: dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus)
- **Pass marks found, licensing context only (src: air_regulations_quick_notes):** the CPL knowledge requirement reads "PASSING MARKS FOR DGCA - 70%". The PPL written exam reads "TECH-GENERAL, TECH-SPECIFIC PASSING MARKS - 70%". The SPL flying-club exam is 50%, and RTR(A) is 50%. These are licence-exam figures from the quick notes. The four syllabus sources do not give the Air Regulations paper's own pass mark, so do not apply these figures to this paper without checking.
- **Not stated in these sources:** number of questions, marks per question, total marks, time allowed, paper split. Do not quote figures for these from memory; check the current DGCA notice.
- The cockpitchats documents say they are not official, and the author is not responsible for loss or misuse (src: regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats_2).

## Syllabus outline by section

### 1. Air Regulations (CAR Appendix C, items 1.1 to 1.15)

Source for the whole block: dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus (Appendix C). Cross-checked against regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats and regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats_2, which give the same headings.

- **1.1 International agreements and organisations**
  - a) Convention of Chicago: sovereignty and territory; non-scheduled flight rights, scheduled services, cabotage, customs airports, applicability of air regulations and rules of the air, search of aircraft; documents to be carried; international standards and recommended practices; endorsement and validity of certificates; notification of differences.
  - b) ICAO: objective and composition; annexes; procedures for air navigation services; regional supplementary procedures; manuals and circulars.
  - c) Other agreements: the five freedoms of the air; Tokyo, The Hague and Montreal Conventions (jurisdiction, PIC authority); Indian organisation (Aircraft Act 1934, Indian Aircraft Rules 1937); Civil Aviation Requirements; Warsaw Convention.
  - d) PIC authority and responsibility for safety and security.
  - e) Operator and pilot liability to persons and goods on the ground.
  - f) Commercial practices and associated rules.
- **1.2 Annex 8, Airworthiness of Aircraft** (applicability).
- **1.3 Annex 7, Aircraft Nationality and Registration Marks** (applicability).
- **1.4 Annex 1, Personnel Licensing** (applicability; relationship between Annex 1 and CARs).
- **1.5 Rules of the Air (Annex 2)**: definitions, applicability, general rules (except water operations), VFR, IFR, signals, interception of civil aircraft, table of cruising levels.
- **1.6 Procedures for Air Navigation, Aircraft Operations (Doc 8168, Vol 1)**: foreword, definitions and abbreviations, departure procedures (general criteria, standard instrument departures, omni-directional departures, RNAV departures, simultaneous operations on parallel runways, use of FMS/RNAV for conventional departures). The cockpitchats version adds altimeter-setting procedures (src: regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats, regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats_2).
- **1.7 Air Traffic Services (Annex 11 and Doc 4444)**: flight contingencies and time in ATS; control of air traffic flow; altimeter setting; indication of heavy wake turbulence; approaches, holding, approach sequence and expected approach time (src: regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats_2).
- **1.8 Aeronautical Information Service (Annex 15 and AIP India)**: definitions and applicability.
- **1.9 Aerodromes (Annex 14, Vol 1 and 2)**: aerodrome data and movement-area conditions; visual aids for navigation (indicators, markings, lights, signs, markers); obstacle marking and lighting; restricted-use areas; emergency and other services (rescue and fire fighting, apron management, ground servicing); Attachment A (declared distances, radio altimeter operating areas, approach lighting systems).
- **1.10 Facilitation (Annex 9)**: definitions; documents including the general declaration; procedures for crew and operator personnel.
- **1.11 Search and Rescue (Annex 12)**: organisation; operating procedures, including PIC duties at an accident scene and when intercepting a distress transmission; search-and-rescue signals (surface craft, ground/air visual codes, air/ground signals).
- **1.12 Security (Annex 17)**: heading only in the CAR outline.
- **1.13 Aircraft Accident Investigation (Annex 13)**: heading only in the CAR outline.
- **1.14 CARs, Sections 2, 7 and 8**: listed Indian Aircraft Act and Aircraft Rules provisions (e.g. Aircraft Rules 1937 rules 1-19, 21-29A and others), Schedules I, II, VI and XI, the Indian Aircraft Rules 1920, the Aircraft Rules 1954 (public health), and the Aircraft Rules 2003 (carriage of dangerous goods).
- **1.15 National law**: national law and differences from relevant ICAO provisions.

### 2. Human Performance and Limitations (CAR items 2.1 to 2.3)

Source for the whole block: dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus. The same headings appear in regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats and _2.

- **2.1 Human factors: basic concepts**: human factors in aviation (competence and limitations; proficiency vs professionalism); flight safety concepts.
- **2.2 Basic aviation physiology and health maintenance**: atmosphere composition, gas laws, oxygen requirement of tissues; respiratory and circulatory systems; pressurisation, decompression, rapid decompression, entrapped gases, barotrauma, hypoxia, time of useful consciousness, hyperventilation, accelerations; high-altitude environment (ozone, radiation, humidity); sensory system, spatial disorientation, illusions, approach and landing problems; health and hygiene (minor ailments, hearing, vision, hypotension, hypertension, coronary disease, obesity, nutrition, tropical disease); intoxication (tobacco, alcohol, drugs, self-medication); incapacitation.
- **2.3 Basic aviation psychology**: human information processing (attention, vigilance, perception, response selection); human error and reliability; decision making (phases, limits, risk assessment); avoiding and managing errors (safety awareness, co-ordination, co-operation, communication, cockpit management); personality and attitudes (hazardous attitudes); human overload and under-load (arousal, stress, fatigue, body rhythm and sleep, circadian rhythm, fatigue and stress management); advanced cockpit automation (advantages, criticalities, automation complacency).

### 3. Operational Procedures and Communications (CAR items 3.1 to 3.3)

Source for the whole block: dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus, with headings also in regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats.

- **3.1 Special and emergency procedures**
  - a) ICAO Annex 6, Parts I, II and III (as applicable): definitions, applicability, framework.
  - b) CAR-OPS requirements: quality system; additional crew members; carriage of persons; admission to flight deck; unauthorised carriage; portable electronic devices; endangering safety; documents and records; leasing; Air Operator Certificate (AOC) issue, variation and validity; operational control; use of ATS; instrument departure and approach; carriage of passengers with reduced mobility, inadmissible passengers, deportees, persons in custody; baggage and cargo stowage; passenger seating; cabin security; smoking on board; take-off conditions; flight and duty time limits and rest.
  - c) Special operational procedures and hazards, including flight preparation, landing in various terrain and water, and passenger information.
- **3.2 Communications (VFR)**: definitions; general operating procedures (transmission of letters, numbers, time, technique; standard words and phrases; call signs; transfer of communication; test procedures and readability scale; read-back); action in communication failure; distress and urgency procedures; relevant weather terms (VFR); general principles of VHF propagation and frequency allocation; Morse code.
- **3.3 IFR communications**: definitions and ATC abbreviations; Q-code groups in air-ground RTF; general operating procedures as in 3.2; action in communication failure; distress and urgency procedures (including PAN medical message); relevant IFR weather terms (aerodrome weather, weather broadcast); VHF propagation; Morse code.

## Study road map

Source: regulations_road_map_1 (SASTA Aviation Academy plan, 20 days, 1-2 hours a day). The plan is a third-party schedule; the disclaimer says it is a basic outline.

1. **Primary reading:** Zenith Aviation regulations notes (about 100 pages, file zenithaviation_regsnotes). Page checkpoints from the plan:
   - Day 1: read to page 17.
   - Day 2: read to page 37 (before the holding pattern).
   - Day 3: read to page 47 (before flight information services).
   - Day 4: read to page 58 (before runways).
   - Day 5: read to page 73 (before human factors).
   - Day 6: read to page 85 (before communications).
   - Day 7: read to page 94 (before Annexure 9, facilitation).
   - Day 8: complete the whole document.
2. **Question bank:** the Indigo question bank, 513 questions. Cumulative targets: Day 1 question 50; Day 2 question 100; Day 3 question 150; Day 4 question 200; Day 5 question 250; Day 6 question 300; Day 7 question 350; Day 8 question 450; Day 9 complete the rest (src: regulations_road_map_1).
3. **Second reading:** Sahil Khurana notes (written notes, same material as Zenith), to be completed in 5 to 6 days (src: regulations_road_map_1).
4. **Third reading:** RK Bali, 14th edition. Read the grey-boxed sections in full; solve the questions for the next chapter after each (src: regulations_road_map_1).
5. **Human performance:** read the whole RK Bali chapter and solve its back questions. The plan says no question bank is needed for human performance (src: regulations_road_map_1).

## Source quality notes

- regulations_syllabus_cockpitchats_2 ends with unrelated promotional text (a rental service and community links). It is not part of the syllabus and was ignored.
- regulations_road_map_1 and the cockpitchats files are third-party study plans, not DGCA documents. The DGCA CAR (dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus) is the authority for scope.
- Annex-number labels in the zenith notes (zenithaviation_regsnotes) do not match the CAR outline. The CAR numbering is used here: Annex 9 facilitation, Annex 12 SAR, Annex 13 accident investigation, Annex 14 aerodromes, Annex 15 AIS, Annex 17 security.
