# HumanFactors

## Definition

- **Human factors (syllabus heading):** "competence and limitations", with the traditional approach to becoming a competent pilot ("proficiency") contrasted with the human factors approach ("professionalism"). (src: dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus)
- **Hypoxia:** "a state of oxygen deficiency in the body sufficient to impair functions of the brain and other organs." (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes). Another source: "a lack of oxygen in the body tissues", caused by a shortage of oxygen in the inspired air or by physiological or pathological problems affecting circulation or oxygen carriage. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Hyperventilation:** "excessive over-breathing", from an increased rate or depth of breathing (or both). (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Spatial disorientation / illusion:** a false sensation from the vestibular system ("false feeling") or the visual system ("false seeing"). (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Decompression sickness:** symptoms caused by nitrogen bubbles coming out of body fluids after a pressure change (Henry's law). (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Barotrauma:** pain from pressure differences, as in the middle ear and sinuses. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Physiological deficient zone:** the zone from 12,000 ft to 50,000 ft, where the body is not used to the environment. (src: air_regulations_quick_notes)

## Classification

- **Four types of hypoxia (NF):**
  1. Hypoxic (altitude) hypoxia: reduced partial pressure of oxygen in inspired air.
  2. Anaemic hypoxia: reduced oxygen-carrying capacity of blood (e.g. carbon monoxide, poor haemoglobin).
  3. Stagnant (hypokinetic) hypoxia: circulatory problems, including blood pooling in the legs under high g.
  4. Histotoxic hypoxia: tissues unable to use oxygen, e.g. due to alcohol, narcotics or poisons.
  (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Four stages of hypoxia (Zenith names; fair gives the altitude bands):** Indifferent; Compensatory; Disturbance; Critical. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, air_regulations_fair)
- **Illusions (NF):** vestibular, visual, autokinetic, somatogyral (angular acceleration), Coriolis, somatogravic (linear acceleration), inversion. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Fatigue and stress (fair):** acute (short term) and chronic (long term). (src: air_regulations_fair)
- **Alcohol and drugs:** an aircrew exclusion (see DGCA Important Facts). (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)

## Formation

- **Atmosphere and gas laws:** Dalton's law: total pressure equals the sum of the partial pressures of the gases. The percentage of oxygen stays the same at all levels, but the partial pressure falls with altitude. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Barometric pressure:** at 18,000 ft the barometric pressure is half the sea-level value. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Oxygen at altitude:** breathing 100% oxygen at 38,000 ft is equivalent to breathing ambient air at about 10,000 ft (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes). Breathing 100% oxygen without pressure at 38,000 ft causes hypoxia. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Oxygen carriage:** oxygen combined with haemoglobin in blood is transported by red blood cells. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Hyperventilation:** causes listed are anxiety, shock, G forces and motion sickness (the source list also has an OCR-garbled entry). Symptoms look like hypoxia. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Decompression:** Henry's law explains nitrogen release in the body. Rapid decompression can cause decompression sickness, especially above 18,000 ft. Diving can produce symptoms below 18,000 ft too. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Health and fatigue:** the DGCA syllabus covers fatigue (types, causes, symptoms, effects), body rhythm and sleep, circadian rhythm, and fatigue and stress management. (src: dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus)
- **Go/no-go check:** the IM SAFE checklist is referenced for the go or no-go decision. (src: air_regulations_fair)

## DGCA Important Facts

- **Alcohol and drugs:** no operating crew member may take alcoholic drink, sedative, narcotic or stimulant within 12 hours of commencement of flight. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, air_regulations_fair)
- **Alcohol gap:** minimum 12 hours between bottle and throttle; the same source writes the limit as below 0.04%. The fair file is OCR-garbled, so treat the 0.04% wording with care. (src: air_regulations_fair)
- **Human factors share of accidents:** "over 73% of accidents" (src: air_regulations_quick_notes). Most crashes happen in the landing phase (src: air_regulations_quick_notes).
- **Medical incapacity:** a sickness or injury involving incapacity of 15 days or more for the licensed work requires a fresh medical before exercising the licence privileges. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Illness reporting:** the holder of a pilot licence must inform the Authority of any illness involving incapacity. (src: air_regulations_4)
- **AME advice:** seek advice from an AME/AeMC if ill for more than 21 days, in hospital for any period, starting a new regular medication, or first needing correcting lenses. (src: air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536)
- **Oxygen threshold (India):** "above 10,000 ft oxygen is required to carry on board." (src: air_regulations_quick_notes)
- **Oxygen threshold (Zenith):** supplemental oxygen is encouraged above 10,000 ft by day and above 5,000 ft at night. CONFLICT with the India line above (requirement vs encouragement, and night threshold). (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Hypoxia stage altitudes (fair):** indifferent to about 10,000 ft; compensatory 10,000 to 15,000 ft; disturbance 15,000 to 20,000 ft; critical 20,000 to 23,000 ft. (src: air_regulations_fair)
- **Time of useful consciousness (fair table, OCR-garbled):** 40,000 ft: 15 to 20 seconds; 35,000 ft: 30 to 90 seconds. (src: air_regulations_fair)
- **Decompression:** a passenger or crew member who has suffered rapid decompression should wait 12 hours before the next flight. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Diving:** do not fly for 12 hours after compressed-air swimming; avoid flying for 24 hours if a depth of 30 ft has been exceeded. Flying immediately after scuba diving is prohibited. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Post-dive rule (VY):** as a general rule, do not fly within 24 hours following diving, and certainly not the same day. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Hyperventilation treatment:** breathe into a paper or taper bag; treat as hypoxia if in doubt, especially at night. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, air_regulations_fair)
- **Flight time limit (cumulative):** 35 hours in 7 consecutive days; 125 hours in 30 consecutive days. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Daily maximum flight time (VY table, OCR-garbled):** 8 hours for the listed crew complement; two-pilot day operations 9 hours up to 3 landings; night 9 hours up to 2 landings. Verify against the CAR before quoting. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Illusion on approach:** upslope makes the pilot feel higher than actual (risk of landing short); downslope makes the pilot feel lower (risk of landing long). (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Autokinetic illusion:** a stationary light stared at for a time appears to move. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Turn illusion:** abrupt head movement during a prolonged constant-rate turn in IMC can cause a false turn sensation. A head turn faster than 30 degrees per second gives a false turn-rate sense. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Somatogravic illusion:** rapid acceleration on take-off gives a nose-up illusion. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Fog and haze:** fog can give an illusion of pitch-up and objects appearing farther away than they are; haze can give the illusion of being high. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)

## Frequently Tested Concepts

- **Hypoxia types:** hypoxic (altitude), anaemic (blood), stagnant (circulation), histotoxic (tissue, e.g. alcohol). Map each cause to its type.
- **Hypoxia stages and altitudes:** use the fair bands (10,000 / 15,000 / 20,000 / 23,000 ft).
- **Hyperventilation vs hypoxia:** the symptoms are similar; treat as hypoxia.
- **Alcohol rule:** 12 hours before flight, for operating crew.
- **Decompression and diving:** 12 hours after rapid decompression; 24 hours after diving.
- **Illusions:** upslope/downslope; fog/haze; autokinetic; somatogravic; somatogyral (turn illusion); vestibular vs visual.
- **Distress phase triggers:** the Human Factors and AccidentsSAR sets share the phase triggers (see AccidentsSAR).
- **Fatigue and cumulative flight time:** 35 h / 7 days; 125 h / 30 days.
- **Medical:** 15 days incapacity; 21 days illness (AME advice).

## Common Mistakes

- **Alcohol window:** 12 hours, not 6, 24 or 36. The rule also covers sedatives, narcotics and stimulants (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- **Hypoxia vs hyperventilation:** the symptoms overlap. At night it is hard to tell them apart; assume hypoxia and treat accordingly (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, zenithaviation_regsnotes).
- **Hypoxia types:** anaemic = oxygen-carrying capacity of blood; stagnant = circulation; histotoxic = tissue use (alcohol, narcotics). Do not give altitude hypoxia to all four.
- **Oxygen threshold:** India notes say oxygen is required above 10,000 ft; Zenith says encouraged above 10,000 ft by day and 5,000 ft at night. Quote the one the question asks for, and flag the difference (CONFLICT).
- **Upslope and downslope:** upslope feels higher than actual (risk: land short); downslope feels lower than actual (risk: land long). Do not reverse them (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh).
- **Decompression wait:** 12 hours after rapid decompression, 24 hours after diving to 30 ft; flying immediately after scuba diving is prohibited.
- **Distress phase:** the distress phase is fuel exhausted, forced landing, or failed comms after alert. The 5-minute rule triggers alert, not distress (see Q53 conflict above).
- **Accident share:** the "73%" figure is a notes statement, not a universal law. Quote it as a source figure.
- **Dalton's law:** the oxygen percentage stays at 21% at all levels; the partial pressure falls with altitude. Do not conclude that the oxygen percentage falls.

## Revision Summary

- Hypoxia has four types: hypoxic (altitude), anaemic (blood), stagnant (circulation), histotoxic (tissue, e.g. alcohol).
- Hypoxia stages (fair bands): indifferent to 10,000 ft; compensatory 10,000 to 15,000 ft; disturbance 15,000 to 20,000 ft; critical 20,000 to 23,000 ft.
- Barometric pressure halves at 18,000 ft; 100% O2 at 38,000 ft is like breathing air at 10,000 ft.
- Alcohol: no operating crew within 12 hours before flight.
- Decompression: 12 hours wait; flying after scuba is prohibited; 24 hours if 30 ft exceeded.
- Hyperventilation: paper bag; treat as hypoxia when in doubt.
- Illusions: upslope = higher (land short); downslope = lower (land long); fog/haze; autokinetic; somatogravic on take-off.
- Medical: 15 days incapacity needs fresh medical; more than 21 days illness needs AME advice.
- Flight time: 35 h in 7 days; 125 h in 30 days.
- Human factors: over 73% of accidents cited; most in the landing phase.
