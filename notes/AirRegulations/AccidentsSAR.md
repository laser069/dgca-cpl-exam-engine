# AccidentsSAR

## Definition

- **Accident:** an occurrence associated with the operation of an aircraft which takes place between the time any person boards the aircraft with the intention of flight until all such persons have disembarked, in which a person is fatally or seriously injured (being in the aircraft, direct contact with any part of it, or direct exposure to jet blast), or the aircraft sustains damage or structural failure. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Serious injury:** injury requiring hospitalisation for more than 48 hours, commencing within seven days of the injury date. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536)
- **Incident:** an occurrence, other than an accident, which affects or could affect the safety of operation. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536)
- **Serious incident:** an incident involving circumstances indicating that an accident nearly occurred. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Search and Rescue (SAR):** the service that arranges prompt assistance to aircraft in distress and their occupants. Contracting states must provide SAR within their territory on a 24-hour basis. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Distress:** an aircraft facing grave and imminent danger and requiring immediate assistance. Signal: radiotelephony "MAYDAY" (say three times). (src: air_regulations_fair, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Urgency:** an aircraft in a situation where there is no imminent danger, but it could become an emergency. Signal: "PAN PAN". (src: air_regulations_fair, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)

## Classification

- **Occurrence hierarchy:** accident > serious incident > incident. Incident is a broad category; serious incident is near-accident (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536).
- **Emergency phases (ICAO):** uncertainty phase (INCERFA), alert phase (ALERFA), distress phase (DETRESFA). Each is declared on specific triggers, below. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, air_regulations_quick_notes)
- **Distress vs urgency signals:** MAYDAY (distress); PAN PAN (urgency). Data-link versions carry the same intent. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **SAR units:** Joint Rescue Coordination Centre (JRCC, both aeronautical and maritime), Rescue Coordination Centre (RCC, a region), Search and Rescue Region (SRR), Rescue Sub-Centre (RSC, under an RCC), Alerting Post (any facility between the reporter and the RCC), and SAR Unit / mobile team that physically carries out the work. (src: air_regulations_fair)
- **Emergency transponder codes:** 7700 (emergency), 7600 (radio failure), 7500 (hijack / unlawful interference). (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, zenithaviation_regsnotes)

## Formation

- **ICAO requirement:** every contracting state must provide SAR facilities for aircraft flying in its airspace, and must have a formal system to alert SAR. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Alerting service:** provided to all aircraft under ATC; to other aircraft with a filed plan so far as practicable; and to any aircraft known or believed to be subject to unlawful interference. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Indian SAR organisation:** National Aeronautical SAR Coordination Committee (NASARCC) chaired by the Secretary, Ministry of Civil Aviation, over land. National Maritime SAR Board (NMSARB) chaired by DG Indian Coast Guard, over oceanic areas. Airports Authority of India (AAI) coordinates land SAR through 4 RCCs at Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai FIRs. Indian Coast Guard runs 3 Maritime RCCs (MRCCs) at Mumbai, Chennai and Port Blair over the high seas. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Satellite SAR:** India uses COSPAS-SARSAT on 406 MHz, with location accuracy normally within 5 km. Two Local User Terminals are at Bangalore and Lucknow. The Indian Mission Control Centre (INMCC) at Bangalore links to the RCCs at Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata and Chennai via AFS network. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **Phase triggers:**
  - Uncertainty (INCERFA): no communication within 30 minutes of when it should have been received (or 30 minutes after a failed call, whichever is earlier); or the aircraft fails to arrive within 30 minutes of its last notified ETA. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, zenithaviation_regsnotes)
  - Alert (ALERFA): after uncertainty, communication still fails; or cleared to land and fails to land within 5 minutes of ETA with no communication; or operating efficiency impaired but no forced landing likely; or known/believed subject of unlawful interference. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, air_regulations_quick_notes)
  - Distress (DETRESFA): after alert, further failed attempts point to distress; or fuel on board is exhausted or insufficient to reach safety; or operating efficiency impaired to the extent a forced landing is likely; or a forced landing is made or reasonably certain. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **PIC duties at an accident or distress scene:** keep the craft in distress in sight until compelled to leave or the RCC says it is no longer necessary; determine its position; report as much information as possible to the RCC or ATS unit. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- **PIC intercepting a distress transmission:** record the position of the craft in distress if given. (src: air_regulations_4)
- **Accident investigation:** the state in which the accident occurs is responsible for the investigation. Other states may take part on request by providing facilities or experts. Purpose: prevent future accidents. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, zenithaviation_regsnotes, air_regulations_4)
- **Notifications and reports:** the state of occurrence notifies the state of registry and the state of operator. The state of design and the state of manufacture are informed as well. Draft final report goes to those states; comments within 60 days; final report released with minimum delay. (src: air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536)

## DGCA Important Facts

- An air accident is reported to DGCA within 24 hours. (src: air_regulations_fair, zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- Incident notification: the PIC, owner, operator and hirer notify the Director-General within 48 hours; the information is sent by the quickest means and in any case within 24 hours of the occurrence. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Stopway use (going onto a stopway) is an incident reported to DGCA within 48 hours. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- Fatal injury: a death within 30 days is classed as fatal. (src: air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536)
- Serious injury: hospitalisation for more than 48 hours within 7 days; also 2nd or 3rd degree burns, or burns over more than 5% of the body. (src: air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536)
- Zenith's accident test: fracture of a bone or 3rd-degree burns, or hospitalisation beyond 48 hours. Example in source: landing in a wheat field and damaging the nose wheel is an accident. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- A missing aircraft is treated as an accident. (src: air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536)
- Accident preliminary report: submitted to the appropriate states and to ICAO. The report must be in one of the ICAO working languages. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, zenithaviation_regsnotes, air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536)
- Final report: released within 12 months of the occurrence; an interim report is required if the final report cannot be issued in time. (src: air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536)
- Report to ICAO: sent where aircraft MCTOM exceeds 2,250 kg (zenithaviation_regsnotes and air_law_keys). A second line in air_law_keys says ">5700kg" for the final report to ICAO. See CONFLICT below.
- State of design or manufacture may participate when all-up weight exceeds 100,000 kg. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes). NASARCC/NMSARB note in notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh refers to a representative for mass over 100,000 kg.
- Distress frequencies: 121.5 MHz and 243.0 MHz (VHF); 2182 kHz (MF); 500 kHz (maritime). Fair also lists 2182/8364 kHz as MF distress. (src: air_regulations_fair, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Alerting phases are timed: 30 minutes (INCERFA), 5 minutes after ETA of landing (ALERFA). (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Urgency (PAN PAN) is not a distress; it is used when there is no imminent danger. (src: air_regulations_fair)
- A PIC who observes that another aircraft or surface craft is in distress keeps it in sight and determines its position (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav). A PIC who intercepts a distress transmission records the position of the craft in distress if given (src: air_regulations_4).
- ELT: an emergency locator transmitter in the aircraft transmits a distress message and is picked up by SARSAT satellites. (src: air_regulations_fair)
- Rules referenced in the notes: Aircraft (Investigation of Accidents and Incidents) Rules 2012 (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).

## Frequently Tested Concepts

- Accident vs serious incident vs incident (the boundary is injury, damage and "nearly an accident").
- Which state investigates: the state in which the accident occurred.
- Phase definitions and their timers: 30 min, 5 min, and the fuel or forced-landing trigger for distress.
- MAYDAY (distress) vs PAN PAN (urgency).
- Emergency transponder codes: 7700, 7600, 7500.
- SAR organisation names: RCC, JRCC, MRCC, RSC, Alerting Post, SAR Unit.
- Satellite SAR: COSPAS-SARSAT, 406 MHz, 5 km accuracy.
- Reporting clock: accident 24 h; incident 48 h (and "as soon as possible" for incidents in VY).
- Aircraft involved in an accident: the PIC must keep the distressed craft in sight and report to RCC.

## Common Mistakes

- **Accident reporting clock:** the accident deadline is 24 hours. Do not reuse the 48-hour figure, which the notes give for incidents and stopway use (src: air_regulations_fair, zenithaviation_regsnotes).
- **MAYDAY vs PAN PAN:** MAYDAY is distress (immediate assistance); PAN PAN is urgency (no imminent danger). The "XXX" signal is also urgency, not distress (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, air_regulations_fair).
- **Phase confusion:** alert phase is apprehension; distress phase is grave and imminent danger with a need for immediate assistance. An aircraft cleared to land that fails to land within 5 minutes is an ALERFA trigger, not DETRESFA (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- **Serious injury threshold:** the test is hospitalisation for more than 48 hours within 7 days, not any hospital visit (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, air_law_keys_notes_230527_222536).
- **Accident vs incident examples:** a broken wingtip in a collision is an accident (pool Q15); a near-collision avoidance manoeuvre is a serious incident (pool Q16). Use the injury/damage test, not the outcome alone.
- **SAR unit names:** JRCC handles both aeronautical and maritime SAR; RCC works within a region; MRCC is the Indian Coast Guard's maritime centre (src: air_regulations_fair, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- **ELT frequency:** 121.5/243 MHz is the VHF distress pair; 406 MHz is the satellite (COSPAS-SARSAT) channel in the Indian SAR notes. Do not treat them as the same system.
- **ICAO reporting threshold:** sources disagree (2,250 kg vs 5,700 kg). Quote the source you are using and mark it as CONFLICT.
- **Pool key vs notes:** a pool question on the alert trigger gives a derived key that does not match the notes (see HumanFactors). Work from the notes and CAR text.

## Revision Summary

- Accident = fatal or serious injury, or damage or structural failure, between boarding (for flight) and all persons disembarked.
- Serious incident = incident where an accident nearly occurred; incident = other occurrences affecting safety.
- Accident report to DGCA within 24 hours; incident within 48 hours (and within 24 hours by quickest means per one source).
- Serious injury = hospitalisation over 48 hours within 7 days; fatal = death within 30 days.
- Phases: INCERFA (30 min), ALERFA (5 min after ETA of landing, or unlawful interference), DETRESFA (fuel exhausted or forced landing).
- MAYDAY = distress; PAN PAN = urgency; SOS appears as distress in one source.
- Indian SAR: 4 RCCs (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai FIRs), 3 MRCCs (Mumbai, Chennai, Port Blair), 2 LUTs (Bangalore, Lucknow).
- Satellite SAR: COSPAS-SARSAT, 406 MHz, within 5 km.
- Distress frequencies: 121.5 and 243.0 MHz (VHF), 2182 kHz (MF), 500 kHz (maritime).
- Investigation: the state of occurrence investigates; preliminary report in an ICAO working language.
