# Aerodromes

## Definition

- **Aerodrome:** "Any area of land (area or space, whether on the ground, on the roof of a building or elsewhere) or water designed, equipped, set apart or commonly used for affording facilities for the landing and departure of aircraft capable of descending or climbing vertically." (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Aerodrome Reference Point (ARP):** "the geographical location of the aerodrome and the reference centre of its traffic zone where an ATZ is established." (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Aerodrome elevation:** "the elevation of the highest point of the landing area." (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Apron:** "a legally defined portion of the aerodrome where passengers, mail and cargo are loaded on to an aeroplane." (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh); zenith adds: "a defined area on a land aerodrome provided for the stationing of aircraft for embarkation and disembarkation of passengers, loading and unloading of cargo, and for parking." (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Maneuvering area:** "that part of an aerodrome provided for the takeoff and landing of aircraft and for the movement of aircraft on the surface, excluding the apron and any part of the aerodrome used for the maintenance of aircraft." It includes runways, taxiways and any defined stopway or pre-take-off area. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Movement area:** "that part of an aerodrome intended for the surface movement of aircraft including the maneuvering area, aprons and any part of the aerodrome provided for the maintenance of aircraft." Buildings and constructed facilities are excluded. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- **Stopway:** "a defined rectangular area on the ground at the end of take-off runs available prepared as a suitable area where an aircraft can be stopped in the case of an abandoned take-off." (src: air_regulations_4)
- **Clearway:** "a defined rectangular area established to permit aircraft to make a portion of its initial climb to a specific height." (src: air_regulations_4)

## Classification

- **Controlled aerodrome:** the notes refer to a "controlled aerodrome" for the signalling-lamp provision and the aerodrome control tower (src: air_regulations_quick_notes, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- **Runway types:** instrument runway (to which instrument arrival and departure procedures apply), with precision and non-precision subtypes. "Non-precision approach runways and precision approach runways CAT I, II and III" are defined as instrument runways. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, air_regulations_4)
- **Parallel runways:** designators supplemented by a letter: "L" and "R" for two parallel runways; "L", "C" and "R" for three; four runways use "L", "R", "L", "R" (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, air_regulations_4).
- **Aerodrome Reference Code (ICAO Annex 14 planning code):** element 1 is a number from the aeroplane reference field length; element 2 is a letter from wingspan and outer main gear wheel span (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, air_regulations_4). Example in source: "2B" (src: air_regulations_4).
- **Rescue and fire-fighting (RFF) category:** based on the overall length of the longest aeroplane using the aerodrome and its maximum fuselage width. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- **Pavement strength:** PCN (pavement) vs ACN (aircraft). An aircraft can safely use a paved area if PCN is equal to or greater than ACN. ACN-PCN reporting applies to aircraft with maximum take-off mass greater than 5,700 kg. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)

## Formation

- Aerodrome standards come from ICAO Annex 14 (Volumes 1 and 2), the DGCA syllabus item "1.9 Aerodromes (Based on Annex 14, Vol. 1 & 2)." Topics: aerodrome data, movement area conditions, visual aids (indicators, markings, lights, signs, markers), obstacle marking and lighting, restricted-use areas, rescue and fire fighting, apron management, declared distances. (src: dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus)
- Visual aids are grouped as: indicators and signalling devices, markings, lights, signs, markers. (src: dgca_regs_cpl_syllabus, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Lighting operation is conditional. Approach lighting is operated: sunset to sunrise; sunrise to sunset when visibility is 3000 m or less; when requested by the pilot; and at other times when desirable for traffic safety. Approach lighting is also operated when the associated runway lighting is operated. (src: air_regulations_quick_notes)
- VASI lights are operated during daylight and darkness. (src: air_regulations_quick_notes, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Runway lighting is not operated if the runway is not in use for landing. Stopway lights operate whenever the associated runway lights are operated. Taxiway lighting is turned on in a sequence giving continuous taxi path indication, and turned off when no longer needed. (src: air_regulations_quick_notes)
- Stop bars: switched on to indicate all traffic must stop; switched off to indicate traffic may proceed. (src: air_regulations_quick_notes)

## DGCA Important Facts

- Aerodrome must have at least one wind direction indicator, visible from aircraft in flight or on the movement area, free from air disturbances. (src: air_regulations_quick_notes, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Landing direction indicator: in the form of a T; orange or white; in a conspicuous place. (src: air_regulations_quick_notes)
- Signalling lamp: provided at a controlled aerodrome in the aerodrome control tower; recommended to produce red, green and white signals. (src: air_regulations_quick_notes, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Runway markings are white. Taxiway markings, runway turn pad markings and aircraft stand markings are yellow. Apron safety lines are red in India. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Runway designation marking: two-digit number at the threshold of a paved runway; on parallel runways it is supplemented with a letter. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Runway centre line marking: stripe plus gap length not less than 50 m and not more than 75 m; each stripe at least equal to the gap or 30 m, whichever is greater. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- Centre line stripe width: not less than 0.90 m (precision approach CAT II and III); 0.45 m (non-precision with code number 3 or 4, and CAT I); 0.30 m (non-precision with code 1 or 2, and non-instrument runways). (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Threshold marking stripes commence 6 m from the threshold. Number of stripes by runway width: 18 m = 4; 23 m = 6; 30 m = 8; 45 m = 12; 60 m = 16. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Runway threshold lights: fixed unidirectional green, at least 6 lights equally spaced. (src: air_regulations_fair, air_regulations_4)
- Threshold identification lights: placed 10 m away from the threshold lights. (src: air_regulations_fair)
- Runway end lights: unidirectional red, at least 6, equally spaced. (src: air_regulations_fair, air_regulations_4)
- Runway centre line lights: over the last 900 m, alternate red and white from 900 m to 300 m from the runway end; all red from 300 m to the runway end. (src: air_regulations_fair)
- Runway edge lights: fixed, variable white. (src: air_regulations_4)
- Taxiway edge lights: fixed, blue. Taxiway centre line lights (other than exit taxiway): fixed, green. (src: air_regulations_4)
- PAPI: consists of a wing bar of 4 sharp-transition multi-lamp or paired units. On or close to the approach slope, the two units nearest the runway show red and the two farthest show white. (src: air_regulations_4)
- 3-bar AVASIS, PAPI and T-VASIS are provided for aircraft whose eye-to-wheel height in the flare is not exceeding approximately 16 m. (src: air_regulations_4)
- Aerodrome usability factor: not less than 95% for the aeroplanes the aerodrome is intended to serve. (src: air_regulations_book_01, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- Runway direction is based toward the prevailing wind. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- Declared distances: ASDA = TORA + stopway (if provided). TODA = TORA + clearway (if provided). (src: air_regulations_4, zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- Minimum obstacle clearance is zero at the departure end of the runway (DER). (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav, air_regulations_4)
- Stopway: width same as runway, all four sides have steady red lights, yellow chevrons, centreline with the runway. Going onto a stopway is an incident reported to DGCA within 48 hours. (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- Obstacles above 150 m within a 15 km radius of the ARP are lit: high-intensity flashing white by day and low-intensity steady red by night. (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh)
- Aerodrome operator designates hot spots on the movement area where necessary. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Aircraft in flight within 2 NM of the nearest point of an aerodrome perimeter must stay above 1800 m (6000 ft) (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes, notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, air_regulations_quick_notes). Aerobatics are not permitted in that zone unless above 1800 m or 6000 ft.
- Zenith gives "Over aerodrome Minimum Height - 6000 feet AGL." (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes)
- Aerodrome traffic lights: a steady green from the tower means clear to land (in flight) or clear for take-off (on ground). A steady red means give way and continue circling (in flight) or stop (on ground). (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav)
- Aircraft on the ground in a collision threat: when two aircraft approach head-on, each stops or alters course to the right. (src: air_regulations_quick_notes)
- Runway-taxiway intersection: runway markings are displayed and taxiway markings interrupted. (src: air_regulations_quick_notes)

## Frequently Tested Concepts

- **Colour code of lights and markings:** runway edge white; taxiway edge blue; threshold green; runway end red; taxiway centre line green; runway markings white; taxiway and aircraft-stand markings yellow.
- **Declared distances:** TORA, TODA, ASDA, LDA. ASDA is TORA plus stopway; TODA is TORA plus clearway. Stopway is for abandoned take-off; clearway allows part of the initial climb.
- **PAPI reading:** on slope, the two units nearest the runway show red and the two farthest show white (src: air_regulations_4). Eye-to-wheel height limit for 3-bar AVASIS / PAPI / T-VASIS: about 16 m.
- **Aerodrome reference code:** the number is from the reference field length; the letter is from the wingspan and outer main gear wheel span.
- **Runway numbering:** two-digit magnetic number (nearest 10 degrees; source OCR is garbled, src: air_regulations_fair), suffix L/C/R for parallel runways.
- **Lighting operation rules:** approach lights by time and visibility (3000 m); VASI day and night; runway lights off if runway not in use for landing.
- **Visual approach:** a flight crew may request a visual approach if the runway is in sight and terrain can be maintained in visual reference (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- **Aerodrome ground signals:** green and red steady or flashing lights from the tower carry different meanings in flight and on ground.
- **Pavement strength:** PCN must be equal to or greater than ACN for safe use of a paved area.

## Common Mistakes

- **Mixing up edge light colours:** runway edge is white, taxiway edge is blue (src: air_regulations_4). Taxiway markings are yellow, not blue.
- **Runway end vs threshold lights:** threshold lights are green and face the approach; end lights are red (src: air_regulations_fair, air_regulations_4).
- **Stopway vs clearway:** a stopway is for an abandoned take-off; a clearway supports part of the initial climb. Do not add clearway to ASDA. ASDA = TORA + stopway; TODA = TORA + clearway (src: air_regulations_4).
- **Runway lights left on:** runway lighting is not operated if the runway is not in use for landing. VASI, by contrast, runs day and night (src: air_regulations_quick_notes, notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- **Approach-light trigger:** the 3000 m visibility threshold applies to daylight. At night, approach lights run from sunset to sunrise (src: air_regulations_quick_notes).
- **Parallel runway letters:** two runways use L and R; three use L, C and R. Four use L, R, L, R (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, air_regulations_4).
- **Code letter vs code number:** the letter is wingspan/outer main gear span; the number is reference field length (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh, air_regulations_4).
- **Apron safety lines colour:** the notes state red in India (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav). A pool question gives a different generic answer. Do not generalise a question's India answer to every aerodrome.
- **Stopway incident reporting:** going onto a stopway is reported to DGCA within 48 hours (src: zenithaviation_regsnotes). Accident reporting is 24 hours (see AccidentsSAR).
- **Over-aerodrome height:** 1800 m (6000 ft) within 2 NM of the aerodrome perimeter; do not confuse with the 1000 ft AGL ADC exemption (5 NM radius), which is an air-defence rule (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).

## Revision Summary

- Aerodrome, ARP, apron, maneuvering area and movement area are defined in ICAO terms in zenithaviation_regsnotes and notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh.
- Markings: runway white; taxiway and stand yellow; apron safety lines red in India.
- Centre-line stripe 50 to 75 m pitch; minimum stripe 30 m; width 0.90 / 0.45 / 0.30 m by category.
- Threshold stripes start 6 m from the threshold; count depends on runway width (4 for 18 m up to 16 for 60 m).
- Lights: threshold green (min 6), end red (min 6), edge white (runway) and blue (taxiway), taxiway centre line green.
- PAPI: 4 units; two red nearest the runway and two white farthest when on the slope.
- Declared distances: ASDA = TORA + stopway; TODA = TORA + clearway.
- Usability factor: 95%. PCN must be at least ACN.
- Aerodrome reference code: number = reference field length; letter = wingspan and outer main gear span.
- Stopway incident is reported to DGCA within 48 hours; accidents within 24 hours.
