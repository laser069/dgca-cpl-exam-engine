## Definition

- The Convention on International Civil Aviation (Chicago Convention) was signed on 7 Dec 1944 in Chicago; it established ICAO (src: air_regulations_quick_notes; notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh).
- ICAO is a specialized agency of the United Nations that coordinates and regulates international air travel (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh).
- The Chicago Convention applies only to civil aircraft, not state aircraft. Aircraft used in military, customs and police services are deemed state aircraft (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- Annexes contain Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPS). PANS are Procedures for Air Navigation Services, issued by the Council (src: air_regulations_fair; notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh).
- Standards: rules every state must follow. Recommended Practices: rules every state should endeavour to follow (src: air_regulations_fair).

## Formation

Background / how it works:

- Chicago Convention was signed by 52 states; it came into effect on 4 April 1947 (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh).
- ICAO has an Assembly, a Council with limited membership plus subordinate bodies, and a Secretariat. The Chief Officers are the President of the Council and the Secretary General (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh).
- Assembly: body of representatives of contracting states; meets every 3 years; sovereign body (src: air_regulations_fair).
- Council: permanent body; responsible to call the Assembly; issues PANS (src: air_regulations_fair).
- Air Navigation Commission (ANC): body of aviation experts; formulates plans and rules; considers and recommends SARPs and PANS for adoption or approval by the Council (src: air_regulations_fair; notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh; air_regulations_quick_notes).
- Headquarters: Montreal, Quebec, Canada (src: air_regulations_fair; zenithaviation_regsnotes).
- Each state's rules over India: Aircraft Act 1934, Aircraft Rules 1937, ICAO rules, amendments. Over the high seas: ICAO (Chicago) rules. Over another country: state of registry rules until they conflict with that country's rules (src: air_regulations_fair).

## Frequently Tested Concepts

- **Freedoms of the air (table):**

| Freedom | Right | Type (src) |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | Fly over a state without landing | Technical (notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav) |
| 2nd | Land for non-traffic purposes (refuelling/repair), no uplift/discharge of traffic | Technical (notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav) |
| 3rd | Put down passengers/mail/cargo taken on in home state | Commercial |
| 4th | Take on passengers/mail/cargo destined for home state | Commercial |
| 5th | Carry traffic between two foreign states on a flight to/from home state | Commercial (notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav) |

- Cabotage: domestic carriage. Each state may refuse other states' aircraft permission to take on passengers/mail/cargo for another point within its territory (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- SARPS = Standards + Recommended Practices (Annexes). PANS = Procedures (Doc 4444, Doc 8168) (src: air_regulations_fair; notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- Who finalises SARPS: ANC considers/recommends; Council adopts or approves (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh).
- Denouncing: Tokyo to ICAO; Montreal 1999 takes effect 6 months after notice is received (src: air_regulations_4 Q10, Q50).
- Which Convention deals with which problem: see Common Mistakes.
- Standards vs Recommended Practices: non-compliance with a Standard must be notified; Recommended Practices are only to be endeavoured (src: air_regulations_fair; air_regulations_4 Q5).

## Common Mistakes

- **Warsaw 1929 vs Montreal 1999 (carriage liability):** air_regulations_quick_notes says Warsaw deals with responsibility for carriage of passengers, baggage and cargo. air_regulations_fair says Warsaw covers compensation for loss of goods and baggage. air_regulations_4 Q21 keys Warsaw; Q49 keys Montreal 1999 for the same subject. Montreal 1999 amended the Warsaw regime (notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh).
- **Montreal 1971 vs Montreal 1999:** Montreal 1971 covers violence/sabotage on board (notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav; quick notes say "Montreal = offences on board leading to damage of property"). Montreal 1999 covers carriage liability (notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh). Same city name, different subject.
- **Tokyo vs Hague vs Montreal 1971:** Tokyo = offences/acts on board against penal law and jurisdiction of state of registration. Hague = unlawful seizure of aircraft. Montreal 1971 = violence/destruction. (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav; air_regulations_quick_notes.)
- **Rome vs Warsaw:** Rome = damage caused by foreign aircraft to third parties on the surface. Warsaw = carrier liability. Do not swap (src: air_regulations_quick_notes; notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh).
- **Rome year:** air_regulations_4 writes "Rome Convention 1933/1952"; notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh gives 7 Oct 1952. Use 1952 for the convention date.
- **Council vs ANC vs Assembly:** ANC formulates and recommends SARPS/PANS; the Council adopts or approves; the Assembly is the sovereign body of representatives (src: air_regulations_fair; notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh; air_regulations_4 Q1 and Q23).
- **Standards vs Recommended Practices:** a Standard is binding unless a national difference is notified; a Recommended Practice is only to be endeavoured (src: air_regulations_fair; air_regulations_4 Q5).
- **PANS vs SARPS:** PANS = Procedures (not Practices) (src: notes_air_regs_nirmal_firosh; notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- **Tokyo denunciation addressee:** "International Civil Aviation Organisation" (air_regulations_4 Q10) vs "all States Members of UN" (distractor option in pool Q14). Only ICAO is the addressee in the source key.
- **1st/2nd (technical) vs 3rd/4th/5th (commercial):** 2nd freedom permits landing only for non-traffic purposes; no uplift or discharge of passengers, cargo or mail (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav).
- **Cabotage:** domestic carriage, not international traffic (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav; air_regulations_4 Q40, Q42).
- **Chicago (signing place) vs Montreal (HQ):** Chicago is where the 1944 Convention was signed; Montreal is ICAO's headquarters (src: air_regulations_fair; zenithaviation_regsnotes).
- **Annex numbers:** Annex 14 = Aerodromes; Annex 17 = Security; Annex 18 = Dangerous Goods; Annex 1 = Personnel Licensing (src: notes_air_regs_vinod_yadav; air_regulations_4 Q28, Q31).
- **Conflicting AIP/Council notice wording:** air_regulations_quick_notes says notify ICAO and publish in AIP; air_regulations_4 Q25 says notify the Council. Use the Council wording when answering the 2nd-part of a question about notification.

## Revision Summary

- Chicago: signed 7 Dec 1944; 52 states; in force 4 Apr 1947; 96 articles; HQ Montreal (src: quick notes, Nirmal, Vinod, fair).
- ICAO bodies: Assembly (every 3 years), Council (36 members, adopts PANS/SARPS), ANC (recommends SARPS/PANS).
- Freedoms: 1st overfly, 2nd technical landing (non-traffic), 3rd/4th/5th commercial; 1st/2nd fall under the Transit Agreement 1944.
- Cabotage = domestic carriage; a state may refuse foreign operators.
- Conventions: Tokyo 1963 (penal offences on board; denounce to ICAO), Hague 1970 (hijacking), Montreal 1971 (violence/sabotage), Warsaw 1929 and Montreal 1999 (carrier liability), Rome 1952 (third-party damage on surface).
- Montreal 1999 denunciation effective 6 months after notification is received.
- Standards are binding unless a difference is notified; Recommended Practices are endeavoured.
- Annex map: 1 Licensing, 6 Operations, 7 Marks, 8 Airworthiness, 11 ATS, 14 Aerodromes, 17 Security, 18 Dangerous Goods, 19 SMS.
- PANS-ATM = Doc 4444; PANS-OPS = Doc 8168.
