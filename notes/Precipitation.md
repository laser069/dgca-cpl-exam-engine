# Precipitation

## Definition
Water in liquid or solid form falling from clouds and reaching (or nearly reaching) the surface,
formed when cloud droplets/ice crystals grow large enough that gravity overcomes updraught and
drag.

## Classification
- **Drizzle** — very small droplets (<0.5 mm), falls from Stratus, often with poor visibility.
- **Rain** — larger droplets; continuous/intermittent rain typically from Nimbostratus or
  Altostratus (frontal, layered ascent); showers from Cumulus/Cumulonimbus (convective).
- **Snow** — ice crystals, forms when the whole column from cloud to ground is below freezing.
- **Sleet / ice pellets** — snow that partially melts then refreezes falling through a
  warm-then-cold layer.
- **Hail** — ice formed by repeated up/down cycling in a CB's strong updraughts, each cycle adding
  a layer of ice; can fall well outside the storm cell, including under the anvil.

## Formation
Two main growth mechanisms:
- **Collision-coalescence** (warm-cloud process): larger droplets fall faster, collide with and
  absorb smaller ones.
- **Bergeron-Findeisen (ice-crystal) process**: in mixed-phase clouds, ice crystals grow at the
  expense of supercooled water droplets because the saturation vapour pressure over ice is lower
  than over water at the same sub-zero temperature — the dominant mechanism for most mid-latitude
  precipitation, even rain (crystals often melt on the way down).

## DGCA Important Facts
- Precipitation type is determined by the temperature profile of the whole column below the
  cloud, not just the cloud-base temperature.
- Fresh snow reflects a very high fraction of solar radiation (~80–90% albedo) — relevant to
  surface heating/cooling and visibility/runway-friction questions.
- On a rainy day, required runway length is generally **more** than on a dry/sunny day (reduced
  braking friction).
- Hail is exclusively a CB (convective) product in the aviation-exam context.

## Frequently Tested Concepts
- Precipitation-type-to-cloud-type pairing (drizzle↔ST, continuous rain↔NS/AS, showers↔CU/CB,
  hail↔CB).
- Effect of precipitation type/intensity on runway performance and required distance.
- Albedo/reflectivity of snow cover.

## Numerical Problems
- Given a vertical temperature profile, determine surface precipitation type (rain vs snow vs
  sleet) by tracking whether the falling hydrometeor spends more of its fall time above or below
  0°C, and whether it re-freezes before reaching the ground.

## Memory Questions
- *Snow surface reflects about ____% of solar radiation:* **~80%**.
- *On a rainy day compared to a sunny day, the runway length required is:* **More**.
- *Drizzle occurs from:* **Stratus (ST)**.

## Common Mistakes
- Assuming cloud-base temperature alone determines precipitation type at the surface — the whole
  column matters.
- Underestimating hail risk outside the visible storm core (under the anvil).

## Revision Summary
Precipitation grows via collision-coalescence (warm clouds) or the Bergeron ice-crystal process
(mixed-phase clouds); surface type depends on the full below-cloud temperature profile; hail is a
CB-only, exam-favourite hazard that can strike outside the visible cell.

## Supplementary (IC Joshi, ch.11 — OCR)

### Droplet-size definitions
- **Drizzle (DZ)** — 0.2–0.5 mm diameter
- **Rain (RA)** — 0.5–5 mm diameter
- **Shower (SH)** — solid/liquid, >5 mm
- **Hail (GR)** — ice balls/pieces, 5–50 mm or more

### Third growth mechanism: Giant Nucleus theory
Alongside Bergeron (ice-crystal) and collision-coalescence, maritime clouds with **limited
vertical development** can still rain because giant hygroscopic nuclei (salt-spray particles)
carried aloft absorb water vapour, form large drops directly, and kick-start the coalescence
chain reaction without needing the cloud to reach the freezing level.

### Cloud → precipitation type (fuller table)
| Cloud | Precipitation |
|---|---|
| ST | Drizzle |
| AS | Rain and snow |
| NS | Continuous precipitation |
| TCU, CB | Rain, showers, hail, snow |
| Fair-weather CU, AC, CI, CC | No precipitation |
| Thick CS | Snow, high-to-medium levels |

### India-specific rainfall patterns
- **Tropics (most of India):** convection peaks in the afternoon → rainfall mostly afternoon/early
  night. **Coastal areas** (land/sea breeze) and **NE India/valleys** (katabatic/anabatic effect):
  maximum rain shifts to night/early morning instead.
- **Extreme NW India:** follows the temperate-latitude pattern instead — Western Disturbances
  (occluded fronts) are more frequent in winter, so this region's precipitation peaks in
  **winter**, unlike the rest of the country.
- **Rainy day** (India Met definition): a day with **≥2.5 mm** rainfall.

### Rainfall intensity classification
Light <7.5 mm · Moderate 7–35.5 mm · Rather Heavy 35.6–64 mm · Heavy 65–84.9 mm ·
Very Heavy ≥85 mm · Exceptionally Heavy ≥250 mm.

### Cloud seeding / fog dispersal
Nucleating agents (silver iodide, common salt, solid CO₂, potassium chloride) are injected to
stimulate the Bergeron process artificially — used against drought, for firefighting support,
harvest promotion, and fog dispersal at aerodromes (short-duration clearing to enable landing/
takeoff). Note: seeding one area's clouds can deprive a downwind area of rain that would
otherwise have fallen there naturally.
