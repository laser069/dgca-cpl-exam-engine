# Clouds

## Definition
Visible aggregates of water droplets and/or ice crystals suspended in the atmosphere, formed when
air is cooled below its dew point (usually by ascent/adiabatic expansion) and condensation nuclei
are present.

## Classification
By height of base (mid-latitude values):
- **Low (surface–6,500 ft):** Stratus (ST), Stratocumulus (SC), Cumulus (CU), Nimbostratus (NS,
  can extend through all levels)
- **Medium (6,500–20,000 ft, "Alto"):** Altocumulus (AC), Altostratus (AS)
- **High (16,500–45,000 ft, "Cirro"):** Cirrus (CI), Cirrocumulus (CC), Cirrostratus (CS)
- **Vertical development:** Towering Cumulus (TCU), Cumulonimbus (CB) — base in the low level,
  tops can reach the tropopause and beyond (overshooting tops into the stratosphere).

Special high-altitude clouds (outside the troposphere):
- **Nacreous (mother-of-pearl)** — upper stratosphere, polar winter.
- **Noctilucent** — mesosphere (~80 km), visible at twilight from high latitudes.

## Formation
- **Convective:** surface heating → rising thermals → cooling to dew point → CU, growing to TCU/CB
  in unstable air with sufficient moisture.
- **Orographic:** forced ascent over high ground → lenticular/cap clouds, standing-wave clouds.
- **Frontal:** widespread ascent of warm air over a frontal surface → layered NS/AS/CS sequence
  ahead of a warm front.
- **Turbulent mixing:** shallow ST/SC in the friction layer under an inversion.

## DGCA Important Facts
- CB is the only cloud type from which **heavy icing, severe turbulence, hail, and lightning**
  are all simultaneously likely.
- Nacreous clouds: upper stratosphere. Noctilucent clouds: mesosphere. (Frequently swapped in
  distractors.)
- Drizzle typically falls from Stratus; continuous rain from Nimbostratus; showers from Cumulus
  family.
- CB anvil can produce hail well outside the visible storm cell — hail under a CB anvil is a real
  hazard even in apparently clear air nearby.

## Frequently Tested Concepts
- Height bands for low/medium/high cloud genera.
- Which cloud type is associated with which precipitation type and which hazard (icing,
  turbulence, hail).
- Cloud amount coding: FEW (1–2 oktas), SCT (3–4 oktas), BKN (5–7 oktas), OVC (8 oktas).

## Numerical Problems
- **Cloud base from surface T/Td (approximate):** base (ft) ≈ 400 × (T − Td in °C) — a rough
  convective condensation level estimate frequently used in problems giving surface temperature
  and dew point.
- **Oktas → METAR group:** e.g. 5/8 coverage → BKN.

## Memory Questions
- *Mother of pearl clouds occur in:* **Stratosphere** (nacreous).
- *Noctilucent clouds occur in:* **Mesosphere**.
- *Drizzle occurs from:* **Stratus (ST)**.

## Common Mistakes
- Swapping nacreous (stratosphere) and noctilucent (mesosphere).
- Assuming CB tops are capped at the tropopause — overshooting tops routinely punch into the
  lower stratosphere.
- Forgetting NS (Nimbostratus) is classified as a low-level genus by base height even though it
  extends vertically through multiple levels.

## Revision Summary
Classify by base height (low/medium/high) plus the vertical-development family (CU→TCU→CB);
formation is convective, orographic, frontal, or turbulent; CB is the single most hazardous cloud
type, combining icing, turbulence, hail, and lightning risk.

## Supplementary — Optical Phenomena (IC Joshi, ch.10 — OCR)
Cloud-recognition-by-optical-effect is a small but real DGCA sub-topic:

- **Halo** — luminous ring, **22° radius** (small halo, most common) or 46° (large halo, less
  bright), around the sun/moon. Formed by **refraction through ice crystals in Cirrostratus
  (CS)**. Mnemonic already used above: *Halo ↔ High cloud (CS)*. Signifies predominance of ice
  crystals → **negligible icing chance**.
- **Corona** — one or more small coloured rings, **radius under 5°**, centred on the sun/moon,
  formed by **diffraction through small water droplets or ice particles in Altostratus (AS)**.
  Duller colours than a halo, red on the outside. Signifies supercooled water → **indicates
  moderate icing** if the cloud is above the freezing level.
- **Rainbow** — 42° radius primary bow (red outside, violet inside), formed by refraction +
  internal reflection in raindrops; a fainter 52°-radius secondary bow (colours reversed) is
  sometimes visible outside it.
- **Bishop's Ring** — faint ring (~22° radius) from diffraction by fine dust (including volcanic
  dust) high in the atmosphere — distinguishable from a halo by cause (dust, not ice) though
  similar radius.
- **Glory** — coloured rings around an observer's own shadow, seen on cloud/fog of small water
  droplets — pilots commonly see this around the aircraft's shadow on cloud tops.
- **Mirage** — bending of light through layers of strongly varying air density. **Inferior
  (lower) mirage** over intensely heated surfaces (steep lapse rate); **superior (upper) mirage**
  over cold surfaces like snow/cold sea (temperature inversion).
